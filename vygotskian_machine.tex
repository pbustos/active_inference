\section{The Vygotskian Machine}
\label{sec:vygotskian}

Vygotsky's account of how higher mental functions develop has one central law: every such function appears twice,
first between people and then within the individual \cite{vygotsky1978mind}. A child plans aloud with an adult before
planning aloud alone, plans aloud alone before planning silently, and plans silently before the plan runs without
words. Language is the vehicle of that passage: the means by which knowledge held by others becomes the learner's
own, and the tool with which the learner regulates its own action while the knowledge is still new.

We propose to build a robot on that law and call it a \emph{Vygotskian machine}. Its higher functions --- goals,
plans, the meaning of a scene, what to expect of a room --- arrive from outside: from the person who lives with it and
from a vision-language model that carries a large share of human culture. They are then internalised: first as an
inner voice that questions, plans and imagines (Section~\ref{sec:inner_speech}), and eventually as priors, counts
and habits that need no language at all. The robot's automatic level --- the Active Inference agents that build its
belief hierarchy and drive it --- is what the internalised knowledge ends up shaping, and what language is recruited
to help whenever that level is out of its depth.

\paragraph{Internalisation in Active Inference.}
The passage from outer to inner has a precise reading in the framework of this document. Culture enters the
generative model in two places: as prior beliefs and preferences --- what a room is for, which objects go together,
what outcomes are acceptable --- and as candidate policies scored by expected free energy. Experience then
internalises it in two corresponding places: the priors are progressively replaced by the robot's own counts, and
the candidate policies that keep succeeding settle into the habit prior over policies. The machine's dependence on
language for routine matters should therefore fall with experience, and remain for what is novel, social or
structural.

\paragraph{Organisation.}
Section~\ref{sec:inner_speech} develops internalisation: the inner voice as epistemic action, the attention modes
that recruit it and release it, and the learning it drives. Section~\ref{sec:lang_hierarchy} develops the cultural
plane: how the VLM is bound to the belief hierarchy in three roles --- culture, sequencing and imagination --- under
the rule that the language model proposes and the generative models dispose.

\paragraph{The internalist contrast.}
The opposing view holds that language evolved first as an internal system for thought and was only later used to
communicate \cite{berwick2016why}. We use it as a contrast rather than a rival: the distinction it draws between the
faculty of language and the content language carries (Section~\ref{sec:inner_speech:why_lm}) is what makes the
Vygotskian claim testable. A machine with a compositional inner language but no culture --- built by library
learning from the robot's own experience alone (Section~\ref{sec:inner_speech:library}) --- is the
\emph{non-Vygotskian control}. If it matches the culture-fed machine on situations far from what the robot has seen,
the culture is not doing the work the name claims.

\paragraph{Prior uses of the idea.}
Mirolli and Parisi argued for a Vygotskian cognitive robotics in which language serves as a cognitive tool as well as
a means of communication \cite{mirolli2011towards}, and Colas et al.\ proposed Vygotskian autotelic agents that
internalise language and culture to imagine and pursue their own goals \cite{colas2022language}. The present proposal
shares their premise and differs in three respects: internalisation is formalised inside Active Inference, as culture
entering priors and policies and settling into counts and habits; the internalised knowledge acts on a probabilistic
belief hierarchy whose evidence rules and cavity messages keep it from confirming itself; and an attention mechanism
decides, moment to moment, whether language is needed at all.

\paragraph{What would show the name is earned.}
Three predictions carry the claim. The non-Vygotskian control falls behind the culture-fed machine in proportion to
the novelty of a situation (P7 in Section~\ref{sec:inner_speech:predictions}). Language use behaves as private speech
does, rising with difficulty and falling with mastery (P8). And the scaffold is withdrawn: as confirmed rooms and
successful missions accumulate, the VLM's calls fall while task success holds (L6 in
Section~\ref{sec:lang_hierarchy:predictions}).
